EchoGuard

Hydrophone buoys hear North Atlantic right whales calling. EchoGuard counts those up-calls hour by hour, forecasts the next 72 hours and turns the forecast into a LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH warning for ships.

Station 2 · Detect

Rising, not loud

An up-call is a sweep that rises through 50–250 Hz in about a second. Ship noise and falling sweeps can be just as loud, so loudness alone fails. EchoGuard's detector reads each 2-second window's frequency contour: the loudest frequency in the band, frame by frame. Try it below; this spectrogram is computed in your browser with a real FFT.

dominant-frequency contour (50–250 Hz)

Detectors on real Kaggle clips

The threshold is set on validation clips so that at least 80% of detections are real calls; precision and recall are then measured on unseen test clips.

Station 4 · Analyse

What kind of series is this?

Before forecasting, the series is tested for stationarity, decomposed and checked for memory. These results decide the model form: no differencing (d = 0) and a 24-hour season.

Autocorrelation, lags 1–72 h

Bars outside the shaded 95% band are real memory. Peaks at 24, 48 and 72 h are the daily cycle repeating.

Station 6 · Warn

The next 72 hours

Risk comes from the risk bound: the 75% quantile of the forecast plus past errors made at the same hour of day. HIGH means at least a 25% chance of crossing the HIGH threshold; a missed whale costs more than an extra alert. Scrub through the hours, play the forecast, or move the thresholds and watch the warnings change.

About this run

What is real and what is simulated

EchoGuard also runs on real buoy data: fetch_whoi.py downloads 15-minute right whale call tallies from a live WHOI Robots4Whales buoy plus NOAA sea-surface temperature, and run_all.py --hourly-csv forecasts them with the same pipeline.
